Sealed sv3a Raging Surf booster boxes are listing as low as $69.99 on TCGplayer as of August 2026, which tells you something important about where singles prices have settled after three years of supply absorption.

This set dropped September 22, 2023, as a Japanese-exclusive enhanced expansion under the Scarlet and Violet era. If you are working through the [japanese pokemon cards value](/japanese-pokemon-cards-value) pillar, sv3a is one of the more tractable sets to understand: compact checklist, well-defined chase tier, and graded-pop data that is still thin enough to matter. This post breaks every chase card by current price, flags the graded opportunity where it exists, and cuts the cards that do not justify the slab cost.

## What makes sv3a a viable collecting target in 2026

Raging Surf released at 180 yen per pack in Japan. That low entry price drove heavy opening, which generally suppresses raw singles values over time. The set has recorded 1,832 total PSA auction sales for a cumulative $69,122 in auction value (per PSA's public data). Average sale per transaction: roughly $37. That is a set where individual highlights matter far more than broad exposure.

The SIR (Special Illustration Rare) slots are where the real price action lives. Everything else in sv3a is filler by comparison.

<Callout variant="info">
Sv3a runs 92 cards total. The SIR slots sit at numbers 077 through 092 on the collector number sequence. If you are photographing a card to price it, you need the collector number off the card face, not just the artwork, to confirm you have a SIR and not a standard ex or standard rare.
</Callout>

## Garchomp ex SIR: the set's sleeper case

Garchomp ex SIR at 082/071 is a card PokeBeard has discussed in the context of sv3a's SIR tier, noting its relatively thin graded population compared to more-hyped SIRs from adjacent sets. Treat that as community sentiment, not a price call. The actual data: raw copies have traded between $95 and $115 through mid-2026, and PSA 10s have cleared in the $280-$320 window.

The grading math at these numbers: PSA standard service runs around $25 per submission in 2026 (based on current PSA Economy tier pricing). On a $100 raw card that grades PSA 10 at $300, you are looking at roughly $175 in net upside before platform fees (eBay takes 12-13%, TCGplayer around 10%). That is a defensible submission if your pull grades clean. Anything with edge wear or surface scratches does not pencil.

## Iono SIR: the real reason people crack sv3a packs

No card in sv3a generates more consistent search volume or completed-sale activity than Iono SIR (091/071). The $180-$220 raw range has held without meaningful compression since early 2025, which is unusual for a Japanese set card three years post-release. Most sv3a commons and uncommons have fallen to under $5 raw. The Iono premium is structural, not speculative.

PSA 10 Iono SIRs have cleared above $520 on multiple completed sales in 2026. The pop remains low relative to the number of packs opened globally because sv3a was a Japan-exclusive release and never received a direct English equivalent (English sets drew from different card pools). That geographic constraint limits the ceiling on raw supply reaching PSA's intake.

<Callout variant="tip">
If you pull an Iono SIR and the card shows no visible print lines or centering issues, submit it. The PSA 10 premium over raw is large enough to justify the wait and submission cost at any current service tier.
</Callout>

## The Iron Bundle SIR: underrated relative to peers

Iron Bundle SIR (086/071) sits at $55-$70 raw in August 2026, which places it below Penny SIR despite comparable artwork quality. The gap is partly explained by Iron Bundle's lower competitive relevance post-rotation: the card has collector appeal but not the tournament demand that props up Iono. PSA 10 comps in the $160-$200 range suggest a narrower spread than Garchomp ex SIR. Grading this card only pencils if you are confident in a 10-grade pull and are willing to hold through a thinner buyer pool.

## Cards that do not clear the grading bar

Everything outside the SIR tier in sv3a is largely a pass for grading consideration. Standard ex cards from sv3a (Garchomp ex base, Palafin ex base, Clodsire ex base) have settled at $3-$8 raw. The PSA submission cost erases any realistic upside on a PSA 10 sale. Buy them raw if you want them for your binder. Skip the slab.

The standard Illustration Rares (IRs) sit in the $8-$25 range depending on subject. Pirate King Investments recently flagged some Komiya-artwork IRs from adjacent sets as undervalued at $8 entry points, and the sv3a IRs follow a similar profile. Worth watching as raw pickups, not as grading targets, at current service pricing.

## How sealed product fits into the sv3a picture

Sealed Raging Surf booster boxes were listed as low as $69.99 on TCGplayer as of August 2026, down significantly from the $138.99 listing at other retailers. The gap between asking price and sold price matters: completed eBay sales for sv3a boxes have come in at varying levels, and the arbitrage between platforms is real if you are sourcing for break-even EV. Opening a box at $70 for EV versus pulling a PSA-grade Iono SIR is a reasonable calculated risk. Opening at $139 is not.

PikaPikaPapa's commentary on sealed Japanese boxes appreciating over time generally applies to high-demand sets with limited supply. Sv3a does not fit that profile cleanly given the heavy opening history and available sealed stock as of mid-2026. Sealed speculation here is a weaker thesis than it is for lower-print sets.
