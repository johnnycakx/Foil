## What Makes sv3a Worth Tracking in 2026

Sv3a is a 92-card mini set anchored by the Water-type Paldea story arc. Released at 180 yen per pack, it was never a deep-print volume set. Booster boxes ran smaller than a standard SV set, and that supply constraint matters in 2026: raw copies of the Special Illustration Rares aren't flooding the market the way sv3 Obsidian Flames cards are.

PSA's published auction data for the 2023 Pokémon Japanese sv3a Raging Surf group shows 1,832 total auction sales with a cumulative value of $69,122. That's a per-sale average just under $38, which tells you the bulk of graded transactions are concentrated in the lower-grade commons and uncommons, not the chase tier. The chase cards pull that average up, but they're a small slice of submissions.

PokeBeard flagged Garchomp ex SIR earlier this summer as a card with underbuild relative to other sv-era SIRs, noting it had been a $5 card at release before the market corrected. That kind of origin story matters: cards that opened cheap and drifted up organically tend to have stickier floors than hype-driven spikes.

## Garchomp ex SIR (085/062): The Set's Anchor

The Garchomp ex Special Illustration Rare is the card that defines sv3a's ceiling. Raw NM copies have traded between $45 and $60 through most of 2026, with brief dips during broader market sell-offs.

The graded story is more interesting. PSA 10 copies cleared $175 in some early 2025 auctions when the set was still actively being cracked, then settled back. A realistic 2026 expectation for a PSA 10 is $130-$160 in a normal week, with $180 as an outlier ceiling when two motivated buyers meet at auction.

The grade premium math: you're paying roughly $50 raw, spending $25-$40 in grading fees (PSA's current tiered pricing as of 2026), and waiting months. A PSA 10 outcome gets you to $130-$160. The upside is real if you have high-confidence NM+ raw stock. If the card has any print line or centering issue, common in Japanese mini sets, the PSA 10 probability drops fast and the grade premium disappears at PSA 9.

## Mew ex SIR (086/062): Narrower Spread, Steadier Floor

Mew ex SIR trades at a modest discount to Garchomp ex, typically $38-$52 raw and $110-$155 PSA 10. Mew has reliable cross-market demand: it pulls buyers from the 151 set fan base, the general Mew collector base, and sv-era Japanese card speculators simultaneously.

PokeBeard mentioned Mew ex SIR this summer in the context of sv-era SIR pricing relative to Destined Rivals chase cards, framing it as a settled market rather than an active opportunity. That's accurate. The card isn't going anywhere dramatically in either direction based on current supply.

One note: because 151 receives so much attention in 2026 (it continues to dominate creator content across multiple channels), anything Mew-adjacent in Japanese sets gets pulled into the comparison. That passive demand is a real price floor support, but it's also why this card rarely dips below $35 raw even in slow months.

## The SAR Tier: Palafin ex and Camerupt ex

This is where the pricing conversation gets more honest. Palafin ex SAR (084/062) sits at $18-$26 raw. It's a genuinely good card aesthetically; the full-art treatment is clean. But Palafin doesn't carry the cross-market name recognition that Garchomp or Mew do, and volume is lighter.

Camerupt ex SAR (083/062) at $8-$13 raw is essentially a low-priority pull. The PSA 10 range of $28-$45 means grading is neutral at best. You're covering fees if you get lucky on the grade and catch a hot auction; you're underwater if you don't.

Neither SAR is a speculator card in August 2026. They're set-completion purchases for sv3a collectors and background inventory for dealers who run Japanese singles.

## Grading Economics: When the Premium Holds in sv3a

<CardScannerEmbed />

Grading sv3a cards makes mathematical sense in one scenario: you have raw NM+ copies of Garchomp ex SIR or Mew ex SIR that you acquired below $40, and you're willing to hold through the turnaround cycle.

The grade ladder for Garchomp ex SIR, as of 2026:

| Grade | Approximate Sale Range |
|---|---|
| PSA 10 | $130-$180 |
| PSA 9 | $55-$75 |
| PSA 8 | $28-$40 |
| Raw NM | $45-$60 |

The PSA 9 is the uncomfortable tier. It often trades below raw NM because buyers discount a graded 9 (they expected a 10) while raw buyers are optimistic about their own ability to grade. This is a known inefficiency in Japanese SIR grading economics. A PSA 8 is worse: you've paid grading fees and created a card that's easier to sell than a raw copy but at a lower effective price.
