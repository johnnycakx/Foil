## What Raging Surf (sv3a) Actually Is

SV3a Raging Surf is a Japanese-exclusive enhanced expansion released September 22, 2023, built around the Paldea beach biome: Garchomp, Palafin, and Water-type supporting cast. It shipped 20 cards per booster box (5-card packs, 4 packs per box) and had no direct English equivalent, which kept sealed supply tighter than main-series sets. By late 2025, sealed booster boxes had drifted down to the $70-$115 range on TCGplayer depending on seller, a meaningful drop from the $140+ launch window.

The set runs 86 cards in the base set plus 92 total slots when you include all parallel rarities: SIR, SAR, Hyper Rare, and Art Rare tiers.

## The Tier-1 Chase: Garchomp ex SIR (#072)

Garchomp ex SIR (collector number 072/086) is the set's anchor. Raw copies in NM condition have traded between $80 and $120 on eBay completed sales in 2026. A PSA 10 commands a meaningful premium: graded examples have cleared $200 to $250 at auction, with outliers on either side.

<Callout variant="info">
The collector number on your Garchomp ex SIR card reads 072/086. If yours reads differently, you may have a parallel variant or a misidentified card from a different SIR slot. Always read name + set code (sv3a) + collector number together; artwork alone won't confirm which print you have.
</Callout>

The grading math here: PSA standard service runs approximately $25 to $30 per card in 2026 (economy tier). If you're buying a raw copy at $90 and paying $28 to grade, your all-in cost is $118 before you know the result. A PSA 9 on Garchomp ex SIR typically clears $130 to $155, which is a thin margin. A PSA 10 at $220 to $250 is where the upside lives, but sv3a Japanese cards are not easy grades. The holo layers on SIR cards pick up scratches in shipping, and Japanese pack-fresh does not mean gem.

## Palafin ex SIR: The Undercard Worth Watching

Palafin ex SIR (073/086) is the set's second SIR and has consistently traded at a discount to Garchomp, roughly 60 cents on the dollar at the raw level. Raw NM copies move around $30 to $50 in 2026. The Palafin ex SIR is arguably the better value buy for a grade-and-hold strategy: the entry cost is lower, PSA 10 pop is not dramatically higher than Garchomp's, and the upside asymmetry is real if any future Palafin competitive play emerges.

PokeBeard flagged the set's secondary SIR cards in recent commentary as an underappreciated segment of the Raging Surf market, framing them as overlooked relative to the Garchomp fixation. That's a fair read on sentiment. Whether it translates to a price move is a separate question.

## Trainer SARs: Misty and Arven

The trainer Special Art Rares in sv3a follow the pattern established across the Scarlet and Violet era: Misty's Pokémon Research SAR (#079) and Arven SAR (#080) both carry collector demand independent of competitive play. Misty cards specifically tend to hold value across years because of character recognition outside the competitive scene.

Misty's Pokémon Research SAR raw NM trades $25 to $45 in 2026. Arven SAR is slightly softer at $20 to $35 raw. Neither of these is a grading target unless you're confident in a PSA 10; the grade premium on $35 raw cards is narrow once you factor in fees.

## The Illustration Rares: Where the Volume Is

Below the SIR/SAR tier, the Illustration Rares (IR) in sv3a are the volume-traded layer. Garchomp ex IR (#068) trades $15 to $28 raw; Palafin ex IR (#069) trades $8 to $15. These are the cards most sellers are moving in 2026 because they're accessible price points with real collector demand.

The IR tier is also where misidentification is most common. Garchomp appears in multiple slots across the set (standard rare, IR, SIR), and sellers occasionally mislabel collector numbers. Always verify: name + sv3a set code + exact collector number. A #068 and a #072 are different products at meaningfully different prices.

A sealed sv3a Raging Surf booster box was listed as low as $69.99 on TCGplayer as of mid-2026 (per the TCGplayer listing data).
